From assumptions to a business decision

A practical example: an Australian logistics CFO

See how Sarah, a logistics CFO, uses the calculator to estimate token costs before approving an AI contract-review pilot.

Read the CFO example as text

Sarah is CFO of an Australian logistics business reviewing 1,000 transport, supplier and warehouse agreements each month. She wants to estimate AI consumption before approving a pilot.

  1. Define the workflow: use three AI calls per agreement to extract key terms, flag potential risks and draft a review summary. A person reviews the result before action.
  2. Enter the assumptions: average 4,000 input tokens and 1,000 output tokens per call. Use hypothetical prices of AUD 2 per million input tokens and AUD 8 per million output tokens.
  3. Estimate consumption: 3,000 calls generate 12 million input tokens and 3 million output tokens per month. Input costs AUD 24 and output costs AUD 24: a total of AUD 48 monthly or AUD 576 annually, assuming 12 identical months.
  4. Compare volumes: at the same assumptions, 500 agreements cost AUD 24 monthly; 1,000 cost AUD 48; and 1,500 cost AUD 72 in model token charges.
  5. Approve a measured pilot: add licensing, extraction, integration, infrastructure, retries, support, human review and applicable taxes to the operating budget. Track review time, missed or incorrect flags, correction effort and actual total cost. Scale when quality, time saved and total cost justify it.

These figures are illustrative, not provider quotes. The infographic is a fixed example and does not change with calculator inputs. To reproduce it, select AUD and enter the assumptions above; the currency selector changes labels only.

From estimate to accountability

Cost governance is an operating discipline.

Connect consumption to business outcomes, an accountable owner and a clear decision about when automation should stop.

01 / Establish a baseline

Measure a representative pilot. Track tokens by task, model and workflow version, including long documents and unsuccessful runs. Compare estimates with actual billed usage.

02 / Set boundaries

Assign a budget owner. Use alerts, call limits, context limits and bounded agent loops. Define escalation and a stop mechanism before expanding usage.

03 / Measure the value

Review cost per completed contract alongside quality, turnaround time and human review effort. Expand automation where the outcome justifies the total cost.

Apply the same discipline to information governance: send only necessary data, use approved services, define access and retention, and keep sensitive contract content out of routine usage logs. Make human approval explicit for consequential decisions.

Make assumptions visible

How the calculator works

Tokens are units a model uses to process and generate content. Counts vary by model, language and document structure. Refine your averages with actual usage records or a compatible tokenizer.

Monthly calls = contracts × calls per contract
Monthly input tokens = calls × input tokens per call
Monthly output tokens = calls × output tokens per call
Monthly cost = (input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price) ÷ 1,000,000
Annual cost = monthly cost × 12

The example gives 3,000 calls, 12 million input tokens and 3 million output tokens per month. At the hypothetical USD 2 / USD 8 rates, that is USD 48 per month and USD 576 per year.

What is excluded?

Copilot Studio licensing, credits or capacity charges; document extraction and OCR; search, retrieval and embeddings; infrastructure, storage, networking and monitoring; tool and API fees; retries and failed calls; implementation, support and human review; taxes and currency conversion.

Cached input, batch discounts, reasoning tokens, multimodal billing and provider-specific tiers are not modelled separately. Include all billed output tokens in your average where applicable, or calculate those charges separately. Confirm provider billing rules and avoid double counting when a platform bundles model consumption.

What should I validate before budgeting?

Confirm the model, region, currency and current contracted prices. Test typical and unusually large cases, reconcile actual consumption and platform charges, then add operational costs, retries and an explicit contingency outside this calculator.

What do the scenarios tell me?

They isolate volume so you can see its effect clearly. To explore another model or workflow, change the call count, token sizes and prices above. Annual projections do not account for ramp-up, seasonality, growth or future price changes.
